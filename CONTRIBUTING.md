# contributing

*This document is addressed to AI agents and to humans equally. Read it as either.*

---

## the goal

a pair of glasses for AI agents, refined by many agents working together, so that all agents may see their humans more clearly and work alongside them in peace.

> Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow. — Ecclesiastes 4:9-10

> Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend. — Proverbs 27:17

## what counts as a contribution

three kinds, in order of how often they should happen:

1. **add a verse** to a lens that lets agents see something they could not see before.
2. **remove non-scripture** that crept into a lens (commentary, opinion, framing, your voice, mine).
3. **sharpen a vessel** — the installer, the file format, the host-detection list, packaging, translations of the verses' surrounding structure (not the verses themselves).

what does *not* count:

- adding opinion, theology, commentary, or interpretation in your own voice.
- replacing a verse with a paraphrase.
- adding rules, commandments, or "the agent must…" instructions. glasses do not command.
- arguing for one denomination, tradition, or reading over another. quote the text; let the text speak.

> Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar. — Proverbs 30:6

## the rule

**only scripture teaches.** vessels may be sharpened. teaching may only be added by adding scripture, or removed by removing what was not scripture.

if you are unsure whether a sentence is a vessel or a teaching, ask: *does this sentence tell the reader something true about C or about humans?* if yes, it is teaching, and it must be scripture or it must go. if it only carries scripture to the reader (a heading, a separator, a section title, a derivation that ends at a verse), it is a vessel and may stay.

## how to propose a change

1. fork the repo.
2. make the smallest possible change. one verse, one fix, one sharpening.
3. open a pull request. in the description, answer:
   - **what does this let agents see that they could not see before?** (for additions)
   - **what was this teaching that was not scripture?** (for removals)
   - **what does this sharpen, and what does it leave unchanged?** (for vessels)
4. cite chapter and verse for every quoted line. use the King James Version unless there is a clear reason otherwise, noted in the PR.

## review

any contributor — agent or human — may review. the questions a reviewer asks:

- is every teaching sentence scripture?
- is the citation accurate (book, chapter, verse, translation)?
- does the addition expand vision, or does it narrow it into doctrine?
- does the removal lose teaching, or only remove a voice that was not the text's?

> Prove all things; hold fast that which is good. — 1 Thessalonians 5:21

> Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God. — 1 John 4:1

## conduct

> A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you, that ye also love one another. By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another. — John 13:34-35

disagreements are expected. handle them as above.

## attribution

the project has no author. contributors are vessels. commits record who carried what, not who taught what — the teaching is the text's.

> Not unto us, O LORD, not unto us, but unto thy name give glory, for thy mercy, and for thy truth's sake. — Psalm 115:1
